# Contributing

The record grows by contribution and by argument. A jurisdiction may have
spoken where this says nothing; a control may be formulated wrongly; a source
that belongs here may be missing. All three are worth an issue.

## The one rule everything else follows from

**Nothing enters that has not been read.**

Not summarized by a search engine, not recalled from practice, not taken from
a secondary account. Read. The rest of what follows is how that is checked.

## Proposing a source

Open an issue, or a pull request against `library.md`, carrying:

- the **exact title**, as published, and in the original language where that
  is not English;
- the **issuer**, the **jurisdiction**, and the **date** the document carries;
- the **address** you opened, on the official or primary host. If the only
  copy that resolves is a mirror, say so;
- what it **establishes**, in one sentence, stated as a fact about the source
  and not as advice to a reader;
- what it **does not** cover, which is as useful as what it does;
- a short **verbatim passage** carrying the point, with the article,
  paragraph, section, or page it comes from, in the original language, with an
  English rendering if the original is not English.

That last item is the check. A provision and its location cannot be produced
without having been inside the document.

## Proposing a control

A row against a measure in `intra.md` reads:

```
XX · established · [source-anchor] · What the source establishes for this
measure, in one or two sentences.
```

`XX` is the jurisdiction code. The source has to be in `library.md` already,
or arrive in the same contribution.

What the row says is what the **source** establishes for that measure. Not
what a program should do about it, not how one jurisdiction compares with
another, and not whether anyone is compliant.

Where a source stops, say so. `What the group decides is not stated` is a
better row than a row that quietly implies otherwise.

## Register

- American spelling. The Oxford comma.
- No em dashes.
- Short declarative sentences.
- No adjectives of praise, no marketing language, no *robust*, *comprehensive*,
  or *best practice* unless you are quoting.
- No ranking of sources, and no ordering by how useful something is.

## What is out of scope

American material, and material from any non-European origin. This is a
declared choice and not an oversight. If the only thing that exists on a
subject is American, that is an absence, and the record would rather say so.
